March 2026 was the month when war rewrote the market playbook. What began as a targeted US-Israel campaign against Iran in late February spiralled into a full-blown regional crisis, with strikes deep into Tehran, Gulf energy assets under fire, the Strait of Hormuz effectively shut and crude oil rocketing to multi-year highs. Back home, HDFC Bank's chairman walked out citing ethical differences, rattling confidence in India's most systemically important private lender. The Fed chose to stay put on rates with an unmistakably hawkish tone, killing any lingering hopes of near-term easing.

The rupee buckled to fresh all-time lows as foreign institutions sold Indian equities at a pace never seen before, while domestic buyers stepped in with equally unprecedented conviction. Just as sentiment approached capitulation, Trump's unexpected diplomatic pivot toward Iran, a 15-point ceasefire proposal and a five-day halt on strikes, sparked a fierce two-day bounce. Whether this marks a turning point or a temporary reprieve remains the central question heading into April.

Broad Market View

March 2026 has unfolded in three distinct phases:

Phase 1 (March 1-18): A steady deterioration driven by escalating Middle East hostilities, rising crude, FII selling and rupee weakness, punctuated by a brief three-session rally (March 16-18) following CLSA’s dismissal of AI deflation fears in IT.
Phase 2 (March 19-23): Renewed carnage as Brent spiked to $119, HDFC Bank’s chairman resigned, the Fed held rates at 3.50-3.75% with a hawkish stance, and Trump issued a 48-hour ultimatum to Iran on March 22nd.
Phase 3 (March 24-25): A sharp relief rally as Trump announced a five-day postponement of strikes, oil plunged 11%, and diplomatic channels opened. The Nifty rebounded ~1.9% on March 24 and gained a further ~1.5% intraday on March 25th, with market capitalization recovering ₹7.25 lakh crore in a single session.

FII outflows have now crossed ₹1,05,205 crore (~$11.2 billion) across 18 consecutive selling sessions in March - a record monthly outflow driven by the energy shock, dollar strength and a repricing of emerging market risk. DII buying of ₹1,19,070 crore has been historic, reflecting the structural strength of India’s domestic capital base - SIP inflows continue to exceed ₹25,000-30,000 crore monthly.

The INR weakened to approximately ₹93.88 per USD, a fresh all-time low. The India VIX spiked above 27, its highest since June 2024.

Theme of the Month: Gold's Worst Month Since 1983 - Why the Safe Haven Failed

Gold plunged over 20% from its March high of ~$5,600 to below $4,200, while a war raged in the Middle East. The explanation is counter-intuitive but critical: gold falls during wars that cause inflation shocks, not recession shocks. Surging oil pushed the Fed to hold rates at 3.50-3.75%, real yields rose to ~1.74%, and the US dollar, benefiting from America's status as a net energy exporter - surged to multi-month highs, making non-yielding gold expensive to hold globally.

Simultaneously, a margin-call cascade forced leveraged traders across asset classes to liquidate their most liquid holdings for emergency cash. Gold, after a 64% rally in 2025, was the obvious target. The Gold ETF recorded a $2.91 billion single-day outflow, the largest since 2016. This is the essential insight for investors: during acute crises, gold's paper price is driven by leverage mechanics and dollar liquidity, not fundamentals. Physical gold premiums remained elevated throughout; central bank buying held steady.

The structural bull case is intact: central bank purchases (~585 tonnes quarterly), expanding fiscal deficits and de-dollarisation trends haven't changed. J.P. Morgan maintains its $6,300 year-end target. Historical precedent supports patience: gold fell 30% during 2008's deleveraging, then rallied 170% over three years.
