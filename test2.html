Broad Market View

The Nifty moved in a narrow range with elevated volatility, as markets remained cautious ahead of the Union Budget. Early gains were limited, while sharp intraday swings emerged closer to Budget Day due to policy-related uncertainty.

Indian equities remained volatile but ended stronger, supported by optimism around the India–US trade deal, which lifted export-linked sectors and strengthened the rupee toward ~₹90.3/USD despite initial pressure from Budget-led STT hikes on derivatives.

The Union Budget 2026–27 reinforces a medium-term growth bias with sustained capex, infrastructure push, SME support and fiscal consolidation, even as near-term market liquidity remains sensitive to tax and compliance changes. Budget-session volatility was primarily driven by the increase in Securities Transaction Tax (STT) and a higher tax burden on F&O traders, which led to unwinding of leveraged positions. Sentiment was further dampened by the absence of strong measures to revive FII inflows, concerns over fiscal discipline and deficit management, and limited clarity on sector-specific incentives and capex allocation, all of which triggered sharp, knee-jerk market reactions.

Q3 earnings continue to be stock-specific, with 1,205 companies reported so far—financials, select autos, consumer and tech showing resilience, while margin pressures persist in cyclical and cost-sensitive sectors, limiting broad-based rallies.

Theme of the Week: INR Outlook FY27

The INR has remained under pressure, depreciating nearly 5% in CY25, which has weighed on sentiment and triggered panic hedging by importers rushing to cover future USD liabilities. This surge in hedging demand has pushed USD/INR forward premiums sharply higher, with the 3-month forward premium rising to around 2.6% from ~1.4% six months ago. As a result, 3-month INR implied yields have moved up to ~6.7%, offering a meaningful yield advantage over comparable Indian PSU CD yields. This widening yield differential has helped cap further downside in short-term onshore INR assets, even as RBI measures allowing delayed export repatriation have added to near-term INR liquidity pressure.

Looking ahead to FY27, while India’s GDP growth remains resilient, external sector pressures such as muted portfolio inflows, soft trade momentum, and earnings downgrades, continue to influence INR sentiment. However, the current account deficit is expected to stay well contained below 1% of GDP, supported by strong services exports and lower oil prices offsetting elevated gold imports. Capital flows are likely to improve in FY27 on expectations of greater global bond index inclusion, allowing INR depreciation to moderate to ~2%, even as the RBI prioritises currency stability over sharp appreciation.

Expert's Opinion

Q&A with S Naren, CIO at ICICI Prudential Mutual Fund

Q: The hike in Securities Transaction Tax (STT) on F&O seems aggressive. Which specific stocks or sectors are most vulnerable to this, and is the impact structural or just sentimental?

"The government's decision to raise the Securities Transaction Tax (STT) on futures to 0.05% and options to 0.15% is a significant structural change intended to curb excessive speculation, but it directly threatens the revenue models of market intermediaries. Companies like BSE Ltd, Angel One, MCX, and CDSL are the most vulnerable because their valuations are closely tied to high trading volumes. If retail participation drops due to the higher transaction costs, these firms could see a contraction in earnings growth. However, this move may have a silver lining for long-term asset managers. Traditional Asset Management Companies (AMCs) such as HDFC AMC or Nippon Life might eventually benefit if retail investors shift their focus from high-risk F&O speculation back to more stable investment vehicles like mutual funds."

Q: With the ₹10,000 Cr 'Biopharma SHAKTI' scheme, does the government favor Biologics over traditional Generics? Which pharma giants are best positioned to capitalize on this?

"The introduction of the 'Biopharma SHAKTI' scheme signals a clear government preference for moving the industry up the value chain from simple generics to complex biologics and biosimilars. This transition favors companies that have already invested heavily in deep science and biology platforms. Biocon and its subsidiary Syngene are perhaps the primary beneficiaries, as their entire business model is built around biologics and research services. Additionally, major players like Dr. Reddy's and Sun Pharma, which have been aggressively pivoting toward specialty biosimilars to combat price erosion in the US generics market, will likely utilize this funding to scale their high-tech manufacturing capabilities."

Q: PSU Banks tanked during the speech. Is this just profit booking, or does the high borrowing target (₹17.2 lakh crore) pose a real risk to their treasury income?

"The sharp drop in PSU Bank stocks is likely a reaction to the government's high gross borrowing target of ₹17.2 lakh crore, which creates a genuine risk for their treasury portfolios. An oversupply of government bonds typically drives bond prices down and yields up; since banks hold vast amounts of these bonds, rising yields force them to book "Mark-to-Market" (MTM) losses, hitting their profitability. However, for long-term investors, this dip might represent a buying opportunity in robust names like SBI or Bank of Baroda. If the credit growth cycle remains strong enough to generate interest income that offsets these treasury losses, the structural story for these banks remains intact despite the immediate fiscal pressure."
