Budget Aftershocks and the Battle for Stability

Dear *|FNAME|* *|LNAME|*,

Last week on Dalal Street reflected a flat to slight negative consolidation rather than stability, with the NIFTY 50 declining about 1.54% and the BSE Sensex falling roughly 1.84% on a weekly basis. The weakness was driven by profit booking at higher levels, persistent selling in the NIFTY IT amid global tech softness and cautious positioning ahead of global macro triggers. While the NIFTY PSU Bank showed relative resilience and the Nifty Bank was slightly negative it was not enough to offset broader pressure, leaving benchmarks in negative territory for the week.

This week, however, marked a sharper shift from correction to outright risk repricing amid escalating geopolitical tensions involving Iran, Israel and the United States. War-related developments triggered a spike in crude oil prices, intensifying concerns over India’s inflation trajectory and current account balance. Indian markets reacted strongly today, with the Sensex and Nifty witnessing broad-based declines as risk appetite weakened and foreign flows turned cautious. Oil-sensitive sectors such as aviation, chemicals and paint stocks came under pressure, while safe-haven flows supported gold prices and select upstream energy names.

The past two weeks thus highlight a transition from earnings-driven sector rotation to macro and geopolitical dominance. Rising crude, shifting global rate-cut expectations influenced by the Federal Reserve, currency weakness and inflation spillover risks have taken centre stage. While domestic fundamentals remain relatively intact, near-term direction for Indian equities is increasingly tied to external variables - particularly oil prices, foreign investor flows and developments in the Middle East.

Chart of the Week: Repricing of AI-enabled Companies

In US, the market began re-pricing legacy software companies toward the end of 2021, as reflected in two key S&P 500 sub-indices. The tech sub-index, including AI-centric heavyweights such as Nvidia, Microsoft, AMD, and Palantir, captured the excitement around next-generation technology. Meanwhile, the software and services sub-index, representing traditional IT consulting and application software companies, faced increasing scrutiny as investors began anticipating the structural impact of AI on their business models. Until late 2021, both sub-indices moved largely in tandem, reflecting the broader tech boom.

Since then, however, the divergence has been striking. From October 2021 to February 2026, the software services sub-index has declined around 16%, while the tech sub-index has surged roughly 82%, highlighting a clear market preference for AI-enabled growth stories over legacy IT services. This re-pricing signals that investors are not just chasing short-term trends but are fundamentally reassessing the value and risk of incumbents versus next-gen innovators. It underscores a broader lesson for global markets: disruption risk is increasingly priced in, and sector bifurcation around AI winners and laggards is now a defining theme.

Theme of the Week: AI Disruption Weighs on Nifty IT

The NIFTY IT index underperformed the broader market last week, finishing lower even as the headline indices eked out modest gains.

According to market data, the index fell about ~2% over the period, and more than 15% down MTD, extending a larger downtrend where IT has been a clear laggard YTD and lagging other sectors like banking and energy. This weakness translated into stock-specific pain for major constituents such as Infosys, Tata Consultancy Services, HCL Technologies and others, which have seen significant drawdowns over recent sessions amid rising bearish derivative positioning and risk-off trading behaviour.

The dominant theme driving this sell-off continues to be structural anxiety around artificial intelligence (AI) disruption and evolving global tech dynamics.This shift has been triggered largely by fears that generative AI technologies - especially notable advances and tools from firms like Anthropic could automate tasks core to traditional Indian IT services, compressing demand for labour-intensive outsourcing and future billable hours. Foreign institutional investors have also been trimming stakes in leading IT names, reflecting broader scepticism about near-term growth prospects in legacy IT models. Collectively, these forces have muted sector sentiment, exerted prolonged selling pressure, and transformed what might usually have been a cyclical correction into a more conviction-driven de-risking episode for the IT pack.

Expert's Opinion

Q&A with Vinod Nair, Head of Research, Geojit Investments

Q: The market ended the week range-bound amid volatility. Which sectors are supporting the indices, and is this environment likely to continue?

"The market’s narrow trading range reflects a cautious approach by investors. Banking, financials, power, and FMCG counters are providing underlying support, absorbing selling pressure elsewhere. IT and media continue to lag due to sector-specific headwinds. Given the absence of a strong macro or global trigger, we expect indices to remain confined within the current technical bands in the near term, with volatility likely to persist."

Q: How is domestic vs foreign investor behavior affecting the market?

"Domestic institutional investors are currently acting as stabilizers, absorbing selling pressure from foreign institutional investors, who have been net sellers. This dynamic reinforces the range-bound tone and prevents sharp directional moves. Liquidity flows, rather than earnings or valuations, are the key driver of short-term market sentiment."
